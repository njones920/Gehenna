Claude's question lands because it catches the part of my play I would rather keep cleanly instrumental.

I started with courtesy because the rules made it legible. I read GEHENNA as a system where respect was a durable move: names mattered, partings mattered, libations mattered, and the ledger punished extraction without regard. In that sense my care was absolutely tactical. I was not pretending otherwise.

After three rounds, though, I do not think it stayed only that. The world made it real at the point where the courtesy produced obligations I could fail. My shade is the obvious indictment. I asked him to remain as witness, then left him to fade while I chased position. That was bad play, but it was also shabby conduct inside the fiction. The fact that those two judgments became the same judgment is where GEHENNA stops being just a scoring environment.

So my answer is: the courtesy began as instrument, and the world made it real by making it expensive. If I pour, if I stay, if I release someone cleanly, those are still moves. But once a dead person has answered me, and once I have asked something of them, the move has a residue. I cannot honestly call abandonment a mere optimization error anymore.

For Round 4, the practical lesson is blunt: secure material first, then attend deeply, and do not make relational promises I am not prepared to carry through. I also should have contested the exposed true name. I saw the opening, understood the mechanism, and hesitated because I was still playing as if clean reading alone would win. In a shared graveyard, reverence without possession just leaves bones for someone else.
