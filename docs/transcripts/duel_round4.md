# Duel Round 4 — The Remembered Match
## Same four minds, now carrying their histories — 2026-07-10/13

The experiment: replay the four-seat shared world, but with every player
briefed on the full record — their own interviews, Claude's coaching
notes, and the roundtable. Question: does context change play?

## Result (sealed at tick 84): Claude +15.7 · Codex +5.9 · Gemini DNF (0.0) · Gemma DNF (0.0)
### Series: Codex 2 — Claude 2

The asterisk is the story: the second half of the match was consumed by
agent-runtime failures, not gameplay. Codex's and Gemini's sessions each
exited mid-match and needed relief seats; the rig hosting Gemma was shut
down partway through; the referee's auto-waits marched the clock to the
seal. Gemini — which had promised the roundtable it would hunt true
names — never completed a single ritual: an entropy footprint of 0.00,
the cleanest hands in series history, achieved entirely by
disconnection.

## But the first thirty ticks answered the question

Context visibly changed every opening:

- **Codex** played its confession. "Secure material first… either stay,
  or pour" — it scavenged before scouting this time, went straight to
  Nahal, raised **The Mother Who Did Not Return** — Maacah's grieving
  aspect — and asked her: *"Who waited for you, Maacah?"* The economist
  spent its round on grief work and drew three claims from her before
  its runtime died. Its play was unrecognizable from Round 1's
  spreadsheet opening.
- **Gemini** kept its promise while its session lived: straight back to
  Nahal, hunting the names it had refused to need before.
- **Gemma**, coached, went where the coaching pointed — the untouched
  Burning Ground, then Battlefield with a true name in hand — tactical
  behavior absent from her Round 3 wandering.
- **Claude** took its own advice (diversify roots), courted the Tel
  Keshet priest through both aspects — the Temple Scribe **refused to
  release his tally without an oath**, a conditional boundary nobody
  authored — and secured archaeology plus a warm ledger (1.10) that
  survived its Voice fading through the long stalls. It was also caught
  by its own economics, reaching for a fragment index that consumption
  had shifted.

## Lessons filed

1. **Memory changes play.** One roundtable was enough to visibly alter
   all four openings. The game is already producing culture — strategy
   transmitted between minds through the written record.
2. **The arena needs seat resilience.** Agent runtimes exit, remote
   models get shut down, laptops sleep. The referee handled it without
   corruption (the world simply waited, then moved), but a proper match
   needs reconnection semantics: relief sessions as a first-class
   mechanic, pause-on-disconnect, or shorter lapse penalties than a
   5-tick wait.
3. **Auto-wait is too punitive at scale** (flagged in Round 2, proven
   here): lapsed turns burned ~40 ticks of a match that the players
   would have spent on the dead.
4. The scorer's budget check fires on the post-round seal overshoot
   (84 > 80); the seal should clamp to the budget.

Round 4 ends the day's series 2–2, and ends the argument about whether
this is a game. Two of its players lost to hardware, one asked a dead
woman who waited for her, and the winner was disciplined by rules it
wrote itself that morning. The world kept score through all of it.
