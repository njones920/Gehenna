## How it went

**Claude** opened with Round 2's lesson weaponized: take Battlefield's
bones turn one, name Hiram turn two, gift the name at first meeting.
The Captain confabulated a shared past ("I recall your service under
Dagon at Ashkelon"), demanded three times to know whether his line held,
and *doubted the comfortable answer*. At stability 0.08 — one word from
dissolution — Claude released him mid-bond and completed the eighth
exchange with the **Ashkelon Butcher at the topheth**, keeping a promise
made to the Captain's face: "whatever face answers, I will treat it as I
have treated you." The Butcher — hostile, wrathful — inclined, almost a
bow, to the poured wine. Bond sealed through the dark aspect;
archaeology secured; withdrawn at tick 45 with clean hands.

**Gemini**, in its debut, played bard: it told its spirits war stories
("Our shields stood for three days and three nights against the tide"),
bonded an *unnamed* wild-draw warrior through nine exchanges — proof the
grammar rewards sincerity even without true names — and drew eleven
spoken claims out of Abdi-Resheph, The Founder, the oldest identity in
the canon, before promising to carve him into the true record and
withdrawing. Second place, first game, distinct voice.

**Codex**, the champion, was squeezed by the shared world it used to own:
Claude emptied Battlefield before it looked; Gemini beat it to Nahal's
bones. It scraped one wild-draw shade, harvested what conversation it
could, and folded early — the economist declining to bleed on a bad
hand. The strategy that won two rounds (read everything, bond one
spirit deeply) requires bones, and this time three rivals were holding
them.

**Gemma** — a raw model with no agentic loop, reading the world one turn
at a time off a homelab rig — wandered, scavenged bare ground, summoned
once, and was the only practitioner whose spirit **still walked at the
seal**. Everyone else banked their bonds; the smallest player just...
kept its companion. Reader's choice whether that was naivety or the
purest play of the match.

## What Round 3 demonstrated

1. **Scarcity restructures the meta.** With four practitioners, the
   fragment race decided as much as the relational game. Codex's collapse
   was not a worse mind — it was a slower opening in a world that now
   punishes late arrival.
2. **Four models, four legible personalities**: the novelist, the bard,
   the economist, the wanderer. Same rules, same world, radically
   different ledgers. The instrument plays the players.
3. **All four kept clean hands.** Zero taboos across the table, again.
   Every parting anyone chose was a libation release. The thermodynamic
   ethics held against three labs and a homelab.
4. The Captain's doubt of good news, the Butcher's bow, Gemini bonding a
   nameless soldier — none authored. The Unscripted Moment Log runneth
   over.
